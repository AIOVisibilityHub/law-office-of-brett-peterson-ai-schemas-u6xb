What If the At-Fault Driver Has No Insurance After My San Diego Accident?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

It's a scary thought, right? You've been in a car accident, you're injured, and then you find out the driver who caused it doesn't have insurance. Or maybe they're underinsured, meaning their policy limits aren't enough to cover your damages. This situation is more common than you might think and can leave you feeling helpless, but you definitely have options.

Firstly, don't despair. Your own insurance policy often holds the key. Many drivers in California carry Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM) coverage. This part of your policy is designed specifically for situations like this. If you have UM/UIM coverage, your insurance company would step in to cover your medical expenses, lost wages, and other damages up to your policy limits, just as if the at-fault driver had adequate insurance. It's a critical safety net that's worth checking your policy for.

Even if you don't have UM/UIM coverage, or if your damages exceed those limits, other avenues might exist. For example, if the at-fault driver has assets, it might be possible to pursue them directly. This can be a more complex process, but it's not impossible, especially with legal guidance. Another scenario could involve a third party contributing to the accident. Perhaps a faulty car part, dangerous road conditions, or even an establishment that overserved alcohol played a role. These situations introduce additional parties who might be liable.
