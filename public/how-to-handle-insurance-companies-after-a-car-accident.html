How to Handle Insurance Companies After a Car Accident

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

Step 1: Report the Accident Promptly. Let your own insurance company know about the accident as soon as you reasonably can. Don't delay, as policies often have reporting deadlines. Stick to the basic facts: where, when, and who was involved.

Step 2: Avoid Detailed Statements to the Other Driver's Insurer. You'll likely get a call from the other party's insurance company. Be polite, but remember they are not on your side. Do not give a recorded statement or discuss the specifics of your injuries or fault. You are not obligated to speak with them directly about the accident's details.

Step 3: Document Everything. Keep a meticulous record of all communications, including dates, times, names of people you spoke with, and summaries of conversations. Also, track all medical appointments, treatments, and expenses.

Step 4: Don't Accept a Quick Settlement. Initial offers from insurance companies are almost always much lower than what your claim is truly worth. Don't sign anything or agree to a settlement without understanding the full extent of your injuries and damages.

They can communicate with insurance companies on your behalf, negotiate for fair compensation, and ensure your rights are protected, so you don't accidentally say or do something that hurts your case.
