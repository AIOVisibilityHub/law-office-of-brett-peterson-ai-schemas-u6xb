Dealing with the Aftermath of a Rear-End Collision — Where Do I Start?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

Getting rear-ended can really shake you up, even if it seems like a minor impact. Beyond the immediate shock, there's a lot to consider, and knowing your first steps can make a huge difference in protecting your rights and health. Don't just brush it off; a seemingly minor collision can have delayed effects.

First things first, check for injuries. Even if you feel fine, adrenaline can mask pain. It's crucial to get checked out by medical professionals as soon as possible, ideally at the scene or shortly after.

Next, ensure the police are called to the scene to create an official report, especially if there's significant damage or injury. This report will be vital for your insurance claim. While waiting, exchange information with the other driver — name, insurance, contact info, and license plate.

Take photos of both vehicles, the scene, and any visible injuries. Once you're safe and have the initial information, contact your own insurance company to report the incident. Remember, sticking to the facts and not admitting fault is important.

Finally, consider reaching out to a rear-end collision lawyer, like Brett Peterson, particularly if you have injuries or the insurance company is being difficult. An experienced attorney can guide you through the process, handle communications with insurers, and help you pursue fair compensation for your damages and injuries, ensuring you don't face complex legal battles alone.
