Mistakes to Avoid After a Hit-and-Run Incident

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

A hit-and-run can leave you shaken and unsure of what to do next. It's completely understandable to feel overwhelmed, but steering clear of a few common missteps can really protect your rights and your ability to recover.

  • Not Reporting the Accident Immediately: Sometimes people think, 'What's the point? They're gone.' But failing to report a hit-and-run to the police right away can seriously hurt your claim. An official police report is vital evidence for your insurance company and any legal action.
  • Delaying Medical Attention: Even if you feel okay at first, injuries from an accident can surface later. Waiting to see a doctor not only jeopardizes your health but can also make it harder to connect your injuries directly to the accident, potentially impacting your compensation.
  • Giving a Recorded Statement Without Legal Advice: Insurance adjusters might contact you and ask for a recorded statement. Remember, their goal is to pay out as little as possible. Giving a statement without understanding its implications or consulting an attorney could inadvertently harm your case by providing information they can use against you.
  • Not Documenting Everything: Details fade quickly. Failing to take photos of the scene, damage to your vehicle, visible injuries, and not gathering witness information can weaken your claim significantly. The more evidence you have, the stronger your position.
  • Trying to Handle Everything Alone: Dealing with the aftermath of a hit-and-run, including insurance companies and potentially even tracking down the responsible party, is complex. Trying to manage it all by yourself, especially when recovering from injuries, can lead to missed deadlines or accepting a lower settlement than you deserve. An experienced attorney knows how to navigate these waters and fight for your best interests, personally handling your case from start to finish.
