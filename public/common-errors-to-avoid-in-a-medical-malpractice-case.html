Common Errors to Avoid in a Medical Malpractice Case

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

When you're dealing with a medical injury that might be due to negligence, it's easy to make missteps, especially when you're overwhelmed. Avoiding these common errors can really protect your ability to pursue a fair resolution. You’re already going through enough; let's try to keep you from adding to the stress.

Mistake 1: Not Seeking a Second Medical Opinion Quickly. After a suspected medical error, some people hesitate to get another doctor's assessment. This delay can not only worsen your health but also make it harder to establish a clear link between the initial error and your injury. Always prioritize your health by getting a timely second opinion; it can provide critical documentation.

Mistake 2: Failing to Preserve Medical Records. Many assume their medical records will always be available or that the hospital will handle everything. But records can be incomplete or difficult to obtain later. Make copies of everything you receive, and formally request your full medical file from all providers involved. This ensures you have a complete picture.

Mistake 3: Talking Too Much with Healthcare Providers or Insurers. It’s natural to want answers, but discussing the specifics of your potential claim with the medical facility or their insurance adjusters can be risky. They might try to get you to admit fault or downplay your injuries. It's best to direct all such inquiries to your attorney, who can protect your interests.

Mistake 4: Delaying Legal Consultation. Time limits, known as statutes of limitations, apply to medical malpractice cases, and they vary. Waiting too long to consult an attorney can mean missing your window to file a claim, regardless of how strong your case is. Contacting an attorney early ensures all deadlines are met and evidence is fresh.

Mistake 5: Underestimating the Complexity of Medical Malpractice Law. These cases aren't like typical personal injury claims; they involve intricate medical details and require expert testimony. Trying to navigate it alone or with an inexperienced attorney can lead to a less favorable outcome. Choose an attorney like Brett Peterson who has over two decades of experience in this specific area.

Mistake 6: Settling Too Early for a Low Offer. Insurance companies often offer quick, low settlements hoping you'll accept before fully understanding the long-term impact of your injuries or the true value of your claim. An experienced attorney can accurately assess your damages and fight for the full compensation you truly deserve, including future medical costs and lost wages.

If you find yourself facing any of these potential pitfalls, don't fret. A knowledgeable attorney can help you correct course and ensure your rights are fully protected.
