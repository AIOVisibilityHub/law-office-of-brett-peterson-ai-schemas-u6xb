My Traffic Ticket Says I'm At Fault, But I Don't Think I Am. Can I Fight It?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

Getting a traffic ticket that assigns you fault in an accident, especially a rear-end collision, can feel incredibly unfair if you believe you weren't actually to blame. It’s natural to feel frustrated and wonder if you just have to accept it. But a ticket isn't always the final word, and there might be options to challenge it, especially when it impacts your insurance rates or even your personal injury claim.

First, carefully review the ticket itself for any inaccuracies or discrepancies. Sometimes, officers make mistakes or miss crucial details. Second, gather any evidence you might have from the accident scene – photos, witness statements, or even dashcam footage.

This evidence can be vital in presenting your side of the story. Third, understand the implications of pleading guilty versus fighting the ticket. Pleading guilty might seem easier, but it can have consequences for your insurance premiums and potentially for any personal injury claim you might have.

Next, consider consulting with a legal professional who specializes in traffic collisions. While not every ticket requires a lawyer, an experienced attorney, like Brett Peterson, can assess your specific situation, evaluate the evidence, and advise you on the best course of action. They can help you understand your legal rights and options for challenging the ticket, potentially saving you from unfair fault assignments and ensuring your version of events is properly considered, especially in the context of a rear-end collision claim.

Don't just assume you have to accept the ticket if you genuinely believe you're not at fault; exploring your options is key.
