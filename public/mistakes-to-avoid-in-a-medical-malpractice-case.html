Mistakes to Avoid in a Medical Malpractice Case

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

When you're dealing with the fallout from suspected medical malpractice, it's easy to make missteps that could hurt your claim. A big one is delaying too long. There are statutes of limitations, which are time limits for filing a lawsuit, and if you miss them, you might lose your chance completely. So, if you suspect something is wrong, don't wait.

Another common mistake is not gathering enough information. You'll need all your medical records, bills, and any other relevant documents. Without these, proving your case becomes incredibly difficult. Keep everything organized, and if you're unsure what you need, a lawyer can help guide you.

Don't fall into the trap of communicating directly with the healthcare provider's insurance company without legal representation. Their goal is to minimize their payout, and anything you say can be used against you. Let an experienced professional handle those conversations for you.

Trying to handle a medical malpractice case by yourself is usually a mistake. These cases are notoriously complex, often involving expert medical testimony and intricate legal procedures. An attorney experienced in medical malpractice, like Brett Peterson, can provide the guidance and advocacy you need to pursue fair compensation.

Lastly, don't underestimate the impact a medical error has had on your life. Many people focus only on immediate medical bills, but malpractice can lead to lost wages, ongoing pain, and a decreased quality of life. Make sure all aspects of your suffering are considered when seeking recovery.
