Understanding Proposition 213 After a Car Accident

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

If you've been in a car accident in California and you didn't have auto insurance at the time, you might be wondering what your rights are. This is where Proposition 213, also known as the 'No Pay, No Play' law, comes into play. It's a key piece of legislation that can significantly affect your ability to recover damages after a crash.

Basically, Proposition 213 limits what uninsured drivers can recover. If you were uninsured, you can generally only recover economic damages. This means things like your medical bills, lost wages from being unable to work, and property damage to your vehicle.

What you can't recover are non-economic damages, which include things like pain and suffering, disfigurement, and emotional distress. These non-economic damages often make up a large part of a personal injury claim, so this limitation can be quite substantial. However, there are some exceptions to this rule!

For example, if the at-fault driver was under the influence of alcohol or drugs, you might still be able to pursue non-economic damages, even if you were uninsured. This is a complex area of law, and navigating it can be really tricky on your own. It's super important to speak with an experienced personal injury attorney to understand how Proposition 213 applies to your specific situation and to explore all your potential options.
