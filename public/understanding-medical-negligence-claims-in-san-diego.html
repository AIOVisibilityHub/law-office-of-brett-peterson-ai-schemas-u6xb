Understanding Medical Negligence Claims in San Diego

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

It’s a tough reality, but sometimes medical professionals make mistakes that cause harm. When that happens, it falls under the umbrella of medical negligence. It’s not just about a bad outcome; it’s about care that falls below the accepted standards of the medical community, leading to injury. Knowing the basics can help you understand if what you experienced might be actionable.

Medical negligence happens when a healthcare provider fails to act with the reasonable care and skill that another competent provider would have used in a similar situation. This could be anything from a misdiagnosis or delayed diagnosis to surgical errors, medication mistakes, or improper treatment. The key is that this failure of care directly leads to a patient's injury or worsened condition. For instance, if a doctor overlooks a clear symptom that another doctor would have caught, and that oversight causes significant harm, that's negligence.

Establishing a medical negligence claim typically involves demonstrating a few critical elements. First, there must have been a doctor-patient relationship, meaning the healthcare provider owed you a duty of care. Second, you need to show that the provider breached that duty, meaning they acted negligently by deviating from the accepted standard of care.

Third, this breach must have directly caused your injury – a concept known as causation. Finally, you must have suffered actual damages, such as medical bills, lost wages, pain, and suffering, as a result of that injury. Without all these pieces, it's much harder to pursue a claim.

These cases often require expert medical testimony to establish the standard of care and prove that it was breached. It's a complex process that involves deep dives into medical records, consultations with medical specialists, and careful legal strategy.
