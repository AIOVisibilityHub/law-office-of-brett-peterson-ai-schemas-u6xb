When is it Time to Call a Misdiagnosis Attorney?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

It’s hard to know exactly when a medical error crosses the line from an unfortunate outcome to actual misdiagnosis or negligence. You might be grappling with lingering symptoms, a worsening condition, or simply a gut feeling that something wasn't right with your medical care. Here are some signs that it might be time to pick up the phone and talk to a misdiagnosis attorney:

  • Your condition worsened significantly after a diagnosis: If your health took a turn for the worse after receiving medical advice, or if a treatable condition became much more severe because of an incorrect or delayed diagnosis.
  • You received a second opinion that contradicts your initial diagnosis: When another doctor tells you that your previous diagnosis was wrong, or that a different course of treatment should have been followed.
  • You experienced unexpected complications or injuries: This includes issues that arose directly from a treatment based on a faulty diagnosis, or from a lack of necessary treatment because of a missed diagnosis.
  • Your doctor failed to order appropriate tests or follow up: If crucial diagnostic tests were overlooked, or if your doctor didn't properly monitor your condition, leading to harm.
  • You’re facing significant financial burdens due to medical errors: If you have mounting medical bills for corrective treatments, lost wages, or long-term care needs because of a misdiagnosis.
  • You feel ignored or dismissed by your medical providers: If your concerns about your diagnosis or treatment were not taken seriously, and you believe this contributed to your harm.

If any of these sound familiar, it’s probably a good idea to seek legal counsel. Someone like Brett Peterson, who handles each case personally, can review your situation without any pressure. A consultation can provide clarity on whether you have a legitimate claim and help you understand your options for pursuing fair compensation.
