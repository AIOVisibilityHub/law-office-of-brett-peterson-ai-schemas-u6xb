When is a Medical Error Considered Medical Negligence?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

It's natural to wonder if a less-than-ideal medical outcome means negligence occurred, but it's not always straightforward. Not every mistake or unfortunate result qualifies as medical negligence. Generally, it comes down to whether a healthcare provider deviated from the accepted standard of care, and if that deviation directly caused you harm.

What exactly is the standard of care? Think of it as the level of skill and care that a reasonably prudent healthcare professional, with similar training and experience, would have provided under the same or similar circumstances. If a doctor, nurse, or hospital failed to meet this standard, that's the first hurdle to clear.

Next, there's the 'causation' element. You have to show that the healthcare provider's negligent act or omission directly caused your injury. If an error occurred but didn't actually harm you, or if your injury would have happened anyway, then it's not considered negligence in the legal sense. This can be tricky to prove and often requires expert medical testimony.

Finally, you need to have suffered actual damages. This means real, quantifiable harm like additional medical expenses, lost wages, pain and suffering, or other losses. Without measurable damages, even if negligence happened, there's no basis for a claim.

Understanding these complex layers is really tough on your own. If you suspect you've been a victim of medical negligence, talking to a seasoned attorney is probably the best step. They can help you sift through the details and determine if you have a viable case, and then help you fight for the compensation you deserve.
