Steps to Take After Suspecting Doctor Negligence

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

Your health is the absolute priority. If you suspect negligence has harmed you, seek care from a different, trusted medical professional. This ensures you get the correct diagnosis and treatment, and it also helps create a new, objective set of medical records. Don't delay this crucial step – it's vital for your well-being and for establishing a clear timeline of events.

Start gathering every piece of paper and digital record related to your care. This includes hospital records, doctor's notes, test results, prescription lists, billing statements, and any communication you had with the medical facility or staff. Make copies and keep them in a safe, organized place. These documents are the backbone of any potential claim, proving what care you received and when.

Begin a detailed journal. Write down everything you remember about the care you received, the alleged negligence, and how it has affected your health and daily life. Include dates, times, names of medical personnel, and specific conversations. Also, log your symptoms, pain levels, and any new limitations you face. Personal accounts can provide invaluable context to your medical records.

It’s natural to want answers, but engaging directly with the healthcare provider or their insurance company about potential negligence can be risky. Anything you say could potentially be used against you. It's best to let an experienced legal professional handle these communications to ensure your rights and interests are fully protected.
