How to Handle the Aftermath of a Medical Misdiagnosis

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Attorneys Web Pages

Step 1: Get a second opinion and proper diagnosis. Your immediate health is the priority. Seek out another medical professional to get a correct diagnosis and start the appropriate treatment as soon as possible. Keep detailed records of all new appointments and diagnoses.

Step 2: Collect all your medical records. This is super important. Gather everything from the initial misdiagnosis — doctor's notes, test results, prescriptions, and billing statements. Also, collect records from the new doctors you’ve seen. These documents will be vital in understanding what happened.

Step 3: Document the impact on your life. Write down how the misdiagnosis has affected you. Think about lost workdays, new medical expenses, pain and suffering, and any changes to your daily life or family situation. Be specific and keep a journal if it helps.

Step 4: Understand the timeline. There are often deadlines for filing legal claims, so it's a good idea to know what they are. This isn't something to rush, but it's important not to wait too long. A lawyer can explain these details to you.

Step 5: Talk to a misdiagnosis attorney. Once you have a clearer picture of your health and some initial documentation, reach out for a free consultation. An attorney experienced in medical malpractice can review your situation, explain your legal options, and help you understand if you have a valid claim. They can handle the complexities so you can focus on healing. Remember, an attorney works for you and will make sure your interests are protected.
